01 — Purpose

Why does "My Courses" exist?

Give every K-12 student access to structured, self-paced learning that complements their school subjects — designed so curiosity drives entry, not teacher assignment. Students own their progress: they choose, they start, they earn.

Student-driven
Browse, choose, and start without needing a teacher to unlock anything. Intrinsic motivation, not assignment compliance.
Progress-visible
Completion and earned badges are always visible. Motivation through ownership and milestone recognition.
Grade-aware
Access and content both adapt by grade band. Students only see what is appropriate for their age — automatically, invisibly.
Complementary knowledge principle
Every course in the Madlen catalog must pass this test: does this give a K-12 student something their school curriculum underserves? Madlen does not compete with MEB subject content (Maths, Science, History). It owns the skill layer that school does not teach — how to think, learn, communicate, and navigate the world. English and AI Literacy are the two founding examples of this philosophy.
02 — Grade Access Rules

Who sees which courses

Course access is hard-filtered by the student's registered grade. Students only see the catalog for their band — they cannot browse other bands. The catalog per band is curated, not exhaustive, to avoid cognitive overload. Each course a student sees should feel like it was made for them.

Why the caps and why these numbers?
Explorer is capped at 4 (not 5) because cognitive load research on ages 6–10 shows 3–4 choices is the sweet spot before decision paralysis sets in. Builder and Pathfinder get 5 — by Grade 5 students have the abstract reasoning to evaluate options. Courses excluded from a band are not "hidden" because they're bad; they're excluded because they require cognitive foundations that develop later (metacognition before age 10, abstract financial reasoning before age 11) or because the most critical version of that content is already covered (Internet Safety in Explorer, AI Literacy in Builder).
Hard filtering rule
Students see only the catalog for their registered grade band. There is no way for a student to browse another band's catalog — not through a filter, not through a settings toggle, not through a URL. The band is set at registration and updated only when a teacher or admin updates the student's grade. This is not a UX restriction — it is a product quality guarantee that every student sees a catalog designed for them.
03 — Interaction Types

Question and activity formats

Nine interaction types are available across the platform. Not every type is appropriate for every grade band. Assignment of types per band is based on motor skill development, reading level, cognitive load, and whether the format can communicate meaningfully at that age without confusion.

Multiple choice
One correct answer from 3–4 options. The baseline format — low cognitive overhead, clear feedback loop.
Explorer Builder Pathfinder
True / False
Binary response. Ideal for Explorer — no reading required beyond the statement, instant clarity.
Explorer Builder Pathfinder
Tap the correct image
Select from visual options. Bypasses reading entirely — essential for grades 1–2 where reading is still developing.
Explorer Builder Pathfinder
Fill in the blank
Complete a sentence with a word or phrase. Explorer uses word-bank assistance; Builder and Pathfinder type freely.
Explorer Builder Pathfinder
Block / word ordering
Arrange word or sentence blocks into the correct order. Explorer uses 3–4 short blocks; complexity scales up.
Explorer Builder Pathfinder
Multiple select
"Pick all that apply." Excluded from Explorer — the concept that multiple answers can be right is cognitively ambiguous for ages 6–10 and leads to frustration rather than learning.
Builder Pathfinder
Drag and drop matching
Match items on the left to items on the right. Excluded from Explorer — fine motor drag precision is unreliable on small screens for ages 6–8.
Builder Pathfinder
Sequence ordering
Put a multi-step process in the correct order. Requires abstract sequential thinking — appropriate from Grade 5 onward when formal operational thought develops.
Builder Pathfinder
Short free-text (AI-graded)
Open-ended written response, graded by Madlen's AI for meaning rather than exact wording. Explorer excluded — writing fluency is still developing. Pathfinder responses can be longer and more analytical.
Builder Pathfinder
How types are assigned within a course
Course designers do not pick types arbitrarily — each type is chosen to match what the module is testing. Vocabulary recall → multiple choice or fill in blank. Conceptual understanding → true/false or multiple select. Procedural knowledge → sequence ordering. Causal reasoning → short free-text. The game engine step (module step 3) uses Madlen's interactive formats rather than these quiz types — these apply specifically to the check-in quiz (step 4).
04 — Course Architecture

Module progression logic

A hybrid model: the first module is always unlocked with zero friction to start. Subsequent modules unlock at a 70% completion threshold — not perfection. Optional bonus content is freely accessible throughout to reward curiosity.

Progression flow
  • ✓
    Module 1 — always open
    No prerequisite. Zero friction to start.
  • 2
    Module 2 — unlocks at 70% of Module 1
    Threshold, not perfection. Keeps momentum.
  • 🔒
    Module 3+ — sequential unlock
    Same 70% rule. Clear visual lock state.
  • ★
    Bonus modules — always open
    Enrichment, not required. Rewards curiosity.
Module anatomy
1Hook / curiosity trigger≤ 60 sec
2Core concept — explanationreading or audio
3Interactive activity or mini-gameMadlen game engine
4Check-in quiz (3–5 questions)completion trigger
★Bonus: go deeper / try in real lifeoptional
The quiz score drives module completion %, which triggers the next module unlock. Step 3 is where Madlen's game engine differentiates from a generic course platform.
Why 70%, not 100%?
Perfection gates kill momentum, especially for younger students. 70% proves understanding without punishing struggle. The student can return and improve later for a higher badge tier — so the incentive to do better stays alive after unlock, not before it.
05 — Progress and Rewards

How completion is recognised

Completion is celebrated at two levels: module-level (small win, keeps momentum) and course-level (real milestone, permanent recognition).

⚡ Module complete
XP points added to student profile. Small animation plays. Progress bar on the course card updates. No certificate — this is a stepping stone, not a destination.
🏆 Course complete
Badge earned + downloadable PDF certificate. Badge lives on student profile permanently. Badge tier (Bronze/Silver/Gold) is based on average quiz score across all modules.
Badge tier system
🥉
Bronze
Average 70–79%
🥈

🥇

Students can retake modules to improve their tier — this encourages mastery without creating pressure at first attempt. The student always keeps the badge from their highest run.
06 — Current Courses

Launch catalog: two founding courses

Both courses are complementary knowledge — not MEB curriculum repetition. They develop skills and awareness that school does not formally teach, making them genuinely additive to a student's education.

Language
English Levels
Structured English proficiency course following a CEFR-lite progression (A1 to B2 across the K-12 scope). Not a replacement for school English — a supplement for real-world language confidence and communication.
Optional placement test: Before entering Module 1, the student is offered a short placement test (10–15 questions, ~5 minutes). If taken, they are placed at the appropriate starting module automatically. If skipped, they start at Module 1. Never mandatory — but always available as a time-saving option for students who already have a foundation.

Content by grade band

Explorer 1–4 Phonics, basic vocabulary, greetings, simple sentences
Builder 5–8 Reading comprehension, grammar, everyday conversation
Pathfinder 9–12 Academic writing, debate vocabulary, exam English (IELTS-adjacent)
Madlen differentiator: Each module uses Madlen's AI to generate personalised practice sentences based on the student's recent activity — not static worksheets.
Future skills
AI Literacy
Teaches students how AI works, where it appears in daily life, and how to think critically about it — without requiring coding. Designed around emerging AI literacy frameworks in Turkey and the EU.
Band restriction: AI Literacy is available to Explorer (1–4) and Builder (5–8) only. A Pathfinder-depth version (AI in careers, ethics, policy, prompt engineering) would need to be designed separately and is a future build priority.

Content by grade band

Explorer 1–4 What is AI? Friendly robots, smart apps in daily life, magic vs machine
Builder 5–8 How does AI learn? Bias, data, what it cannot do, responsible use
Madlen meta-moment: At a specific point in this course, students use Madlen's AI assistant to complete a task — then the course explains what just happened. "You just gave a prompt to a large language model." That recognition is something no textbook can replicate.

What we intentionally do not expose
No "difficulty" filter. No "grade level" filter. No way to see that you are in a band at all. Students should simply see a well-curated list of courses made for them — not a system sorting them into levels.
09 — Design Principles

Grounding decisions

1
Complementary, not competing
Every course must teach something school does not. Madlen cannot win a content war with MEB-aligned publishers. It wins at the skill layer — learning, thinking, communicating, navigating — that school leaves to chance.
2
Curiosity drives entry, not obligation
This section is student-browsed, not teacher-assigned. Every design decision — from the hook in Module 1 to the badge tier system — must serve a student who chose to be here voluntarily.
3
Invisible differentiation
Grade bands are real and important, but students should never see themselves sorted into a difficulty tier. The system does the work; the student just sees a catalog that feels made for them.
4
70% unlocks momentum
Progression thresholds should enable learning, not gate it. 70% proves understanding. Perfection requirements create anxiety and abandonment — especially in younger grades.
5
Interaction type follows learning objective
Question formats are not chosen for variety — they are chosen because they best test the specific thing a module is trying to prove. Vocabulary recall needs fill-in-blank. Procedure needs sequence ordering. Causal reasoning needs free-text. The format is always in service of the learning goal.
6
The AI should be part of the learning, not just the delivery
In English Levels, it personalises practice. In AI Literacy, it becomes the subject itself. Every future course should ask: how does the AI make this learning experience impossible to replicate on paper?
