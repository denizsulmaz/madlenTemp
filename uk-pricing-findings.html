Madlen UK Pricing Findings

What UK and international teacher tools charge, how Madlen teachers actually use the toolbox, and the three paid individual plans plus free trial that follow from both. Updated with the decisions taken on 10 September.

Updated 10 Sep 2026 Scope B2C page only. Schools stay on /uk/schools with the pilot form. Status Local draft, iterating with the team Linear MAD-7147 · MAD-7140 · MAD-7138

Decisions so far

  1. AudienceThe pricing page is B2C only. Schools get one short section that links to the existing For Schools page and its pilot request form. No school price is published.
  2. TiersThree paid individual plans. No free tier. Differences are tool availability and generation limits.
  3. TrialA free trial replaces freemium. Proposed: 14 days of Everyday, no card required, then the account goes read-only until a plan is chosen.
  4. Everyday priceMatch Teachmate: £6.99 a month or £59 a year. A percentage market-entry discount applies for the first year.
  5. Entry planThe lowest paid plan (Starter) takes over the role the free tier had: capped generations, small media allowance, PDF export. Numbers in section 4.
  6. LocksWeekly Lesson Plan is in every plan. Locked on Starter: Educational Videos, Video Solutions, Digital Twin, Pronunciation Assessment. Video tools are Complete only. Podcast and Presentation Generator are limited, not locked.
  7. LimitsNothing is unlimited. Every plan has a hard monthly cap on every metered thing; higher plans simply have higher caps. No fair-use wording anywhere.
  8. ShippingStays local through a few team cycles. No waitlist CTAs yet.

1What the UK market charges

Every UK reference price a teacher will compare us against sits between £4.49 and £10.99 a month for an individual, and annual plans cluster at £59 to £72. Above roughly £10 a month the buyer stops being the teacher and becomes the school.

Observed 10 Sep 2026 from vendor pages. Twinkl and Teachit tier prices sit behind JavaScript checkouts and could not be confirmed.

a

Aila sets the reference price for lesson planning at £0. About 30,000 teachers use it, Oak received up to £2m of DfE money for it, and it states it exceeds the DfE's January 2026 AI safety standards. An EEF trial reports autumn 2026.

So: with no free tier, planning alone will not make a teacher pay. The trial and the Starter plan have to lead with what Aila cannot do: marking, presentations, audio, video, images, exam-board alignment for IGCSE and A-level.

b

UK products gate by tool access and export format, not by credits. Teachmate, Twinkl and Teachit all do this. Only Chalkie and Carousel print quotas.

So: a teacher reading "40 generations a month" is reading something slightly unfamiliar. It needs a cadence and a reason next to it, and PDF-only export on Starter is a gate UK teachers already understand.

c

Charm pricing everywhere on the consumer side. £4.49, £4.90, £5.99, £6.99, £8.49, £9.99, £10.99. Annual plans are sold as a per-month equivalent with the total underneath.

So: £6.99 and £59 are exactly the shape teachers expect. The third tier should end in .99 too.

d

Adoption is high, spend data is missing. Teacher Tapp: 58% of teachers used AI for school work in the past week (Oct 2025). NEU: 76% use it day to day (Feb 2026). But only 24% had ever used a specialist AI brand and most brands are recognised by under 10% of teachers. No UK survey measures how many pay personally.

So: the market is generic-tool users who have never paid for a specialist tool. The trial has to convert them from ChatGPT, not from Teachmate, which is why it should be the full Everyday experience rather than a cut-down one.

e

Timing. Self-pay peaks late August to September and again in January. Report writing spikes May to July, and about a third of teachers already use AI for reports.

So: the launch discount should be live for January, and a report-writing tool is worth surfacing before May.

f

The dominant pattern is a hard count on Free and "unlimited" on paid. Eduaide 15 a month, Brisk a daily cap, MagicSchool an undisclosed cap. History and export are the second and third levers. Model-quality gating is rare.

So: we differ twice from the norm, no free tier and no "unlimited". The trial has to do the free tier's job in 14 days, Starter has to be cheap enough that "trial ended" does not mean "back to ChatGPT", and Pro's caps have to be high enough that a teacher never thinks about them.

g

Trials convert better than freemium. Published benchmarks: freemium 3 to 5% good and 6 to 8% great; free trial 8 to 12% good and 15 to 25% great; reverse trial around 15%. No-card trials get more starts, card-required trials convert a higher share of starters.

So: the decision to skip a free tier is supported by the data. Recommend 14 days of Everyday with no card required at launch, since UK teachers are new to paying for specialist tools and the top of the funnel matters more than trial-to-paid right now. Revisit card-required once there is UK conversion data.

h

Two individual tiers exist where the top one buys a quota, not "more unlimited". Chalkie Complete doubles the resource quota. Twee Everyday removes text limits and doubles audio credits. Gamma and ElevenLabs sell tiers of monthly credits with rollover.

So: every tier is defined by counts. Starter has small ones, Everyday comfortable ones, and Complete adds the video tools on top of the largest allowances.

i

Starter at 40 generations a month covers about 95% of in-season teachers. It is the honest light-user plan: p90 is 22 to 24, so almost nobody on Starter hits the cap in a normal month, and the ones who do in August are exactly the ones Everyday is for. At $0.035 a generation a maxed-out Starter costs about $1.40 in text against £3.99 inc VAT.

j

Everyday at 150 generations a month is one and a half times the p99. Fewer than one teacher in a hundred would ever touch it, yet it is a real number a teacher can read. A Everyday teacher who uses every one of the 150 costs about $5.25 in text tokens against £6.99 inc VAT (about $7.80 ex VAT). A typical Everyday teacher at the p75 costs under $0.50 a month. Complete at 400 is four times the p99 and, at $14 worst case against £12.99, still inside margin.

k

Media is where cost is invisible and value is highest. Podcasts, music, images and now video have no USD in PostHog. Keep media allowances small on Starter, meaningful on Everyday, generous on Complete, and read the provider bills after the first UK term.

Caveat. This is Turkish B2B usage where the school pays. UK teachers who have chosen to pay will be a smaller, keener cohort and will probably look more like the August column. Treat every number here as an opening position and re-derive after one full UK term.

4The three plans

Complete is for the teacher who uses video, audio and images as a matter of course, and it anchors Everyday as the sensible middle.

Naming

Pro and Max were working names. Both are taken in this exact market: Chalkie sells Pro and Max, Teachmate sells Pro, Twinkl sells Ultimate and Premium. The research on tier names says value words beat generic intensifiers, and a name that says what the plan is for does the comparison table's job before the reader gets there.

Two rules make this legible. Nothing is invisible: locked tools show a blurred preview and an upgrade prompt, so the user always sees what is one step away. And every limit is a number with a cadence, "2 podcasts a month, resets on the 1st", never the word "limited".

Nothing is unlimited. Each plan is a set of monthly allowances and the next plan up simply has bigger ones. Everyday's 150 generations sit above the p99 of real usage, so the cap is real but almost never felt; Complete's 400 is for the teacher who runs whole departments' worth of material. Marking is the plan's real selling point in the UK, so its ladder is the steepest: 10, 50, 200 papers a month. It is also the one allowance that can outrun its price. At about $0.35 a paper, a teacher who marks all 50 on Everyday costs $17.50 against £6.99, and all 200 on Complete costs $70 against £12.99. That is acceptable only because a full class set is 30 papers and very few teachers mark more than two or three sets a month with the tool; in the Turkish data marking is under 1% of generations. Watch the marking cost per Complete subscriber from the first month, and if the p90 climbs past about 80 papers, either lower the cap or price a marking top-up.

5Rules of the road

Consumer prices include VAT

The DMCC Act 2024 and CMA guidance require the total price including tax in any consumer offer, and the annual total must be as prominent as the per-month figure. Show "£4.92 a month, £59 billed yearly, inc VAT" with both numbers the same size.

Price as if VAT applied, even before registration

The UK entity does not have to register for VAT until £90,000 of UK turnover. Set £6.99 as the inc-VAT price from day one. Otherwise registration later means either a 20% headline rise or a 17% margin cut.

Refund and cancellation

Consumer Contracts Regulations give a 14-day cooling-off right. Teachmate sells it as "14-day refund if you have not used Pro tools". Match it and say "cancel any time".

British English

Marks not grades, marking not grading, year groups, schemes of work, Head of Department. Simon Hay's point: one American term on a pricing card and trust drops.

Copy rules

  • Every plan sells a number. Everyday's headline is "150 generations, 50 papers marked, 20 presentations a month", not a promise of "unlimited". A concrete allowance is easier to trust and easier to defend than a fair-use policy.
  • Starter never says "limited". Numbers with cadence.
  • The trial is always "14 days of Everyday", never "free plan" or "free version", so nobody arrives expecting freemium.
  • Argue from the product, not competitors. The Aila FAQ is the one exception and stays respectful.

7Open items

  • Top-tier price. Complete at £12.99 and £109 is the proposal; £11.99 and £99 is the fallback.
  • Discount depth. 25% off the first year on annual plans is the recommendation; 20% and 30% are tabled.
  • What happens at the cap. Hard stop with an upgrade prompt is the simplest. The alternative is a small top-up pack (say 20 generations for £1.99), which Descript and ElevenLabs do and which keeps a Starter teacher from churning in a heavy month. Decide before the plan model in MAD-7140 is fixed.
  • Video and marking allowances. 8 videos a month on Complete, and 10 / 50 / 200 marked papers across the tiers, are placeholders until per-video cost and UK marking volumes are known.
  • Real media cost per unit. Podcasts, tracks, images and videos have no USD in PostHog. Needs the Gemini, ElevenLabs and image and video provider invoices for one month.
  • VAT registration at launch. Decides whether £6.99 is booked as £6.99 or £5.83 plus VAT internally. Pricing is unaffected either way.
  • Trial mechanics. No card at launch is the recommendation; card-required converts more starters but loses top of funnel. Either way MAD-7140 needs a trial state and a timed move to read-only, and a decision on whether trial history stays visible after expiry (recommended: yes, it is the strongest reason to come back and pay).
  • Starter price and caps. £3.99 and £35 with 40 generations is a first proposal. The alternative is dropping Starter's caps to the old free-tier numbers (15 generations) at £2.99, which makes Everyday look even better but risks feeling mean for a paid plan.
  • Referral. Ece's proposal (referred teacher gets the launch price, referrer gets 7 extra days of trial or Everyday) fits and can follow once prices settle.
  • Data gap. No UK survey measures how many teachers pay for AI tools personally. One Teacher Tapp custom question would settle it.
